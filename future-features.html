Ideas worth validating before we spend heavily building them.

VerseBattles has room to grow into stronger discipleship, review, and ministry-support features. But some ideas are large enough that they should earn partner buy-in before they become roadmap commitments. This page exists to make those possibilities visible and test whether real people want them enough to pilot, sponsor, or help shape them.

Concepts we could pursue with the right backing

These are examples of strategic capabilities that could strengthen long-term Scripture formation and ministry usefulness, but only if real users and partners are willing to help validate them.

Per-user review system

A future review engine that knows which verses each learner is due to revisit and guides them back at the right time.

View the concept page →

Adaptive mission handoff

Missions that hand players into specific review or learning follow-up based on what they struggled with, not just what they played.

Status: concept only

Group study cohorts

Small-group or church cohort flows where leaders can coordinate review rhythms and shared challenges around the same themes.

Status: concept only

What kind of buy-in we are actually looking for

Encouragement is nice, but these features only become justified when someone is ready to validate them in the real world.

Pilot access Let us test the concept with a real ministry, school, or group instead of guessing in isolation.
Testing commitment A serious feature needs users willing to try it, report friction, and tell us if it genuinely helps.
Sponsorship Larger systems can justify engineering time if someone believes enough in the value to help fund validation and implementation.
Strategic critique We want informed pushback, especially from leaders who know what would and would not work in ministry settings.

Start with the clearest concept.

The strongest current future-feature pitch is the per-user Scripture review system. It addresses a real problem: helping learners return to verses before memory fades, instead of only celebrating a one-time session.
